Notebook vs Apple Notes: Notes That Work Everywhere

Updated March 7, 2026

Apple Notes is a solid, well-designed note-taking app that comes free with every Apple device. If you live entirely within the Apple ecosystem, it works beautifully. But what if you also use Windows at work, Android on a tablet, or Linux on a personal machine? That is where Apple Notes falls short — and where Notebook offers a genuine alternative.

Notebook is a free online notepad that works in any browser on any operating system. There is no Apple ID required, no iCloud dependency, and no ecosystem lock-in. Your notes live in your browser's local storage and never leave your device.

Where Notebook Wins

Cross-platform freedom. This is the biggest difference. Notebook works on any device with a web browser. Switch from a MacBook to a Windows desktop to a Chromebook — your memo app works the same everywhere. Apple Notes locks you into Apple hardware.

No account required. Apple Notes needs an Apple ID and iCloud. Notebook needs nothing. Open the page, start writing. There is no sign-up flow, no email verification, no password to forget.

Better export options. Apple Notes makes it surprisingly difficult to export your data. You can share individual notes as PDFs, but there is no bulk export to standard formats. Notebook lets you export all notes as JSON or download individual notes as Markdown files — portable formats you can use anywhere.

No ecosystem dependency. If you ever switch away from Apple, your Apple Notes become hard to access. Notebook's notes are stored in standard formats and can be exported anytime. No vendor lock-in, ever.

Where Apple Notes Wins

Deep OS integration. Apple Notes integrates with Siri, Spotlight search, the share sheet, widgets, and the Lock Screen. This level of system integration is impossible for a browser-based tool.

Apple Pencil support. On iPad, Apple Notes offers excellent handwriting and sketching with the Apple Pencil. Notebook is text-only.

iCloud sync. Your notes automatically appear on every Apple device. Notebook stores notes in a single browser's localStorage — no cross-device sync.

Collaboration. Apple Notes supports shared notes and folders through iCloud. Notebook is a single-user, single-device tool by design.

Scanning and attachments. Apple Notes can scan documents, attach images, and embed links with previews. Notebook focuses purely on text-based notes.

The Platform Question

The core decision comes down to this: are you fully committed to the Apple ecosystem? If every device you own is from Apple and you plan to keep it that way, Apple Notes is deeply integrated and works seamlessly. But if you use even one non-Apple device — a Windows work computer, an Android phone, a Chromebook — Apple Notes becomes a limitation. Your notes are trapped on Apple's platforms.

Notebook eliminates this problem entirely. A web browser is the most universal platform there is. Your notes work on every operating system, every device, every screen size. And because they are stored locally, they are also completely private.

Who Should Use Which?

  • Choose Apple Notes if you only use Apple devices, want handwriting support, need collaboration features, and are comfortable with iCloud storage.
  • Choose Notebook if you use multiple platforms, want complete privacy, prefer no-account simplicity, or need standard export formats for your notes.
